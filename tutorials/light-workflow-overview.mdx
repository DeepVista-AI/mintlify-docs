## Why speed to lead isn't enough anymore

For over a decade, GTM teams built their inbound playbooks around one metric: **speed to lead**, the time between a buyer raising their hand and a rep's first response. The urgency was real. HBR research found that contacting a prospect within five minutes makes them **21x more likely** to enter the qualification stage than contacting them 30 minutes later.

But the metric broke under its own success. Instant auto-responders gamed it, and buyers learned to spot a canned reply and ignore it. At the same time, buyers started expecting messaging that reflects their actual tech stack, recent company moves, and business model, something no two-minute template can fake. That leaves reps choosing between two bad options:

- **Fast but empty**: a generic reply in 2 minutes, low reply rate because there's nothing in it.
- **Relevant but late**: 25 minutes across LinkedIn Sales Navigator, Apollo, press releases, and CRM logs for one tailored email, by which point response time has stretched past 2 hours and the lead has moved on.

## Speed to context: the metric that replaces it

**Speed to context** is the time between a buyer signal and the complete assembly of action-ready account intelligence: ICP fit, company updates, tech stack signals, past interaction history. That assembly needs to happen before the next human interaction, not only the first reply. The same standard applies to post-discovery-call follow-up and account reactivation.

## Three motions, one flywheel

This tutorial series builds the three connected motions behind speed to context, closing the loop between them:

- **Pipeline Control**: one tracking record that updates itself, so nothing gets lost between touchpoints
- **Outbound Motion**: deep account understanding at scale, turned into a daily workflow that finds and reaches new accounts on its own
- **Inbound Motion**: speed to context at the moment of signal, so reps follow up while interest is still fresh

Each motion feeds the next: the tracker gives outbound research something to build on, outbound activity and your own call notes keep that tracker current, and a complete tracking record means an inbound lead who reaches out later is recognized instantly, closing the loop.
